Lab analysis

Tested where the standards were written.

Every commercial batch is analysed at India's two leading aromatics research institutions before it is committed for export.

CSIR‑CIMAP, Lucknow

The Central Institute of Medicinal and Aromatic Plants — the Council of Scientific & Industrial Research laboratory that has authored most of the published analytical work on Indian aromatic oils for the last half century. CIMAP is where our specialty natural lots, including ruh khus, davana, and the floral absolutes, are profiled.

FFDC Kannauj

The Fragrance & Flavour Development Centre at Kannauj — a Ministry of MSME body established specifically to support the Indian fragrance trade. FFDC is where our mint, citronella, lemongrass, and palmarosa lots are profiled, alongside compliance testing for IFRA categories and EU 26 allergen declarations.

What we test for

Each Certificate of Analysis includes:

§
i.

Compositional analysis

GC‑MS profile with named compound identification, retention indices, and full constituent quantification — not just a flame‑ionisation chromatogram.

ii.

Physico‑chemical parameters

Specific gravity, refractive index, optical rotation, solubility profile, acid value, ester value, and flash point per ISO and IS standards.

iii.

Adulterant screening

Screening for known adulterants in each oil family — including DEP, DOP, mineral oil, cedarwood, and synthetic substitutes — with the limit of detection documented.

iv.

Heavy metals (ICP‑MS)

Lead, cadmium, mercury, arsenic, plus aluminium, iron, and copper from storage migration. Critical for cosmetic and food‑contact applications.

v.

Pesticide residue

Multi‑residue pesticide screening on request — standard for our floral absolutes and any oil destined for the EU cosmetics market.

vi.

Regulatory compliance

IFRA category compliance statement, EU 26 allergen declaration, REACH preregistration confirmation, and material safety data per GHS.

Storage

The standard the European houses use, applied at the Indian source.

Most quality loss in essential oils happens between distillation and shipping — not at extraction. We have built our storage protocols around the failure modes that actually compromise oils in the Indian trade.

i

Food‑grade SS 304 drums, not aluminium.

Aluminium reacts with the sesquiterpene alcohols in many Indian oils, slowly elevates acid value, and migrates into the oil over weeks. Aluminium is the industry default; we don't use it. All Sevana stock sits in SS 304 food‑grade stainless steel drums, sized for the lot.

ii

Nitrogen‑flushed headspace.

Oxidation is the single biggest enemy of essential oils in storage — particularly for citrus, citral‑rich grasses, and oils with terpene aldehydes. Every drum is sealed at 90–95% fill with the headspace displaced by inert nitrogen before the closure goes on.

iii

PTFE‑gasketed seals.

Standard rubber gaskets absorb volatile oils and degrade over months. We use only PTFE (food‑grade Teflon) gaskets that are inert against the full essential oil spectrum and do not transfer flavour or odour from previous use.

iv

Dark, temperature‑stable storage.

Stock is held at 15–22°C in a dark room. Where stock is being aged deliberately — ruh khus, sandalwood, vetiver — the same conditions are maintained for the full aging period and certified on the COA.
